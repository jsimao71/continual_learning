\begin{abstract}
We study three linked phenomena: how depth changes the stability and correctness of a response under irrelevant input variation, how the self-attention (SA) window limits transport of predictive context, and how heads contribute to stability and class discrimination. The primary instruments are custom causal Transformers trained on known synthetic distributions. Dataset V2 eliminates singleton and proper-subset shortcuts and crosses six balanced predictive formats with four nuisance levels. Across three seeds, mean held-out generator accuracy is 0.885--0.913, 89.5\% of identities reach stable top-1 behavior, and 70.3\% of nested cases reach a stable long-rule override. A separate nested-pattern bridge finds causal reuse only under competence: for same-target extensions, training raises the nested-minus-nonequivalent update advantage from 0.002 to 0.122, and embedded-short update donors cause less JS damage (0.063 bits) than unrelated same-target (0.079) or nonequivalent donors (0.161). Refinement, override, and hierarchy variants in that broader matrix remain near chance and are not interpreted. A span--distance--window experiment finds that generic inference-time restriction harms full-trained models, but selectively blocking nuisance keys preserves perfect accuracy and reduces margin variance by 40.5\%. SA and FF both change variance substantially, and median cross-layer repair fraction is 0.812. Cumulative Jacobian--vector products are directionally informative but not accurate global surrogates. The supported theory is composed, anisotropic, competence-gated predictive refinement---not semantic abstraction, universal denoising, a sufficient $Lw\ge s$ law, generic ``less is more,'' or globally linear dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the three phenomena}
Let an input be $x=(g,d)$, where $g$ is the predictive structure and $d$ is constructed nuisance, with
\begin{equation}Y\perp d\mid g.\end{equation}
We ask whether depth organizes the many surface realizations $(g,d_i)$ into a stable predictive class while retaining distinctions between different $g$.  This system-level question is investigated in dependency order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{layer composition}: does stability improve, and is the trajectory monotone?
\item \emph{SA transport}: can decisive context reach the prediction position through a finite window?
\item \emph{heads}: which parallel SA channels stabilize a class or distinguish classes?
\end{enumerate}
Continuous learning is not the experimental objective.  Pretrained and prior mechanistic results are retained as secondary external-validity evidence in the appendices.

\section{A pedagogical theory of predictive ensembles}
For a pre-norm block,
\begin{align}r'_\ell&=r_\ell+A_\ell(r_\ell),&r_{\ell+1}&=r'_\ell+M_\ell(r'_\ell).\end{align}
The key point is that layer $\ell+1$ acts on the state produced by layer $\ell$; layers are composed transformations, not independent estimates.  For family $g$, imagine a cloud
\begin{equation}\mathcal E_\ell(g)=\{r_\ell(g,d_i)\}_{i=1}^m.\end{equation}
Its centroid $\mu_{\ell,g}$ describes the common state and covariance $\Sigma_{\ell,g}$ describes realization-sensitive spread.  Applying the unembedding at every depth gives distributions $p_\ell(\cdot\mid g,d)$.  We measure
\begin{align}
D_{\rm within}(\ell)&=\mathbb E_g\mathbb E_d[JS(p_\ell(\cdot\mid g,d),\bar p_{\ell,g})],\\
D_{\rm between}(\ell)&=\mathbb E_{g\ne g'}[JS(\bar p_{\ell,g},\bar p_{\ell,g'})],\\
R_\ell&=\frac{D_{\rm between}(\ell)}{D_{\rm within}(\ell)+\epsilon}.
\end{align}
A useful layer trajectory lowers within-family fluctuation without erasing target signal or between-family separation.  Large $R_\ell$ is not self-interpreting when its denominator is nearly zero, so we always report accuracy, target probability, and numerator/denominator with it.

\subsection{Correctness-aware order parameters}
Let $z_\ell(Y)$ be the correct-target logit and let $z_\ell(c^*)$ be the strongest competing logit. Define the per-realization margin
\begin{equation}m_\ell(g,d)=z_\ell(Y)-z_\ell(c^*),\end{equation}
which is positive exactly when the target is top-1. Across nuisance realizations, the signal and fluctuation are
\begin{align}S_\ell&=\mathbb E_d[m_\ell(g,d)],&N_\ell^2&=\operatorname{Var}_d[m_\ell(g,d)],\\
\Gamma_\ell&=\frac{S_\ell}{\sqrt{N_\ell^2+\epsilon}},&
\Gamma_\ell^{\rm robust}&=\frac{\operatorname{median}_d m_\ell}{1.4826\operatorname{MAD}_d(m_\ell)+\epsilon}.
\end{align}
We always show $S_\ell$ and $N_\ell^2$ beside $\Gamma_\ell$: a large ratio can otherwise arise from a collapsed denominator.  Correctness and class organization are complementary: $\Gamma_\ell$ asks whether the externally specified answer wins reliably, whereas $R_\ell$ asks whether families separate. In representation space we use
\begin{equation}\chi_\ell=\frac{q_\ell}{M_\ell^{\rm class}+\epsilon},\quad q_\ell=\mathbb E_g\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma_{\ell,g},\quad M_\ell^{\rm class}=\mathbb E_{g\ne g'}\|\mu_{\ell,g}-\mu_{\ell,g'}\|^2.\end{equation}
The first top-1 depth is the first boundary where $m_\ell>0$; stable depth is the first boundary after which it remains positive. Never-correct trajectories use a missing sentinel, never the final layer.

For boundary-to-boundary progress $a_{\ell i}=m_{\ell+1,i}-m_{\ell,i}$, write $a_{\ell i}=\mu_\ell^\Delta+\epsilon_{\ell i}^\Delta$. Then
\begin{align}S_L^{\rm cum}&=\sum_\ell\mu_\ell^\Delta,\\
(N_L^{\rm cum})^2&=\sum_\ell(\sigma_\ell^\Delta)^2+2\sum_{\ell<k}\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_\ell^\Delta,\epsilon_k^\Delta).
\end{align}
This decomposition makes repair visible: negative cross-layer covariance means that fluctuations introduced at one boundary tend to be counteracted later.

\subsection{Why non-monotonicity is expected}
For a perturbation $\delta r_\ell$, linearize one block:
\begin{equation}\delta r_{\ell+1}\approx J_\ell\delta r_\ell,\qquad J_\ell=I+\frac{\partial F_\ell}{\partial r}.\end{equation}
After $L$ blocks the relevant map is the product $J_{L-1}\cdots J_0$.  One factor may expand a nuisance direction and a later factor may rotate or contract it.  Hence final contraction does not imply contraction at every layer.  We test the approximation with Jacobian--vector products (JVPs), never by constructing a dense Jacobian.

\subsection{Context as a causal cone}
With a local window $w$, one layer can move information at most $w$ positions in the mask graph.  Thus $Lw\ge s$ is a necessary reachability condition for a source $s$ positions away in the simple mask used here.  It is not sufficient: attention weights, width, optimization, and intermediate coding can still prevent useful transport.

\subsection{Heads as vector-field components}
Write $A_\ell(r)=\sum_h A_{\ell h}(r)$.  For head $h$, define
\begin{align}U^{\rm stab}_{\ell h}&=D^{(-h)}_{\rm within}-D^{\rm intact}_{\rm within},\\
U^{\rm disc}_{\ell h}&=D^{\rm intact}_{\rm between}-D^{(-h)}_{\rm between}.
\end{align}
For two heads, $\Gamma(h,k)=U(h,k)-U(h)-U(k)$ distinguishes synergy ($\Gamma>0$) from redundancy or compensation ($\Gamma<0$).  These are causal, task-conditioned quantities; an attractive attention motif is not a substitute.

\section{Controlled methods}
The generator records predictive family, held-out surface identity, target, pattern tokens/length/span, nuisance tokens/count/type/seed, answer-changing status, continuation entropy, training frequency, split, and generator seed.  Group~1 uses contiguous n-grams, skip-grams, and binary functors: nine families, three model seeds, nuisance counts 0/4/8, twelve realizations, width 48, four layers, and four heads. At the initial pre-SA state and every post-SA/post-block boundary we retain target logit/probability/rank/margin, strongest competitor, top-1 result, entropy, distribution hash, residual covariance, first/stable decision depth, reversals, layer-update signal/noise, and cross-layer covariance. Group~2 uses eight $(s,w,L)$ cells, four predictive classes, an exactly tested local mask, and a separately labelled local-pattern task.  Group~3 uses a trained four-layer/four-head full-attention model and evaluates every head with zero ablation, batch-mean replacement, same-family replacement, mismatched replacement, and every within-layer pair.  Manifests store resolved configs, seeds, data identities, and checkpoint hashes.  Family/seed bootstrap is used for the confirmatory Group~1 contrasts; head scans are descriptive and no row-level significance is asserted.

Dataset V2 strengthens the first group. Tokens are partitioned into role vocabularies but nuisance uses the same vocabulary as predictive tokens, preventing a trivial vocabulary filter. Balanced pair, three-token, sparse-pair, functor, short nested, and long nested rules have target entropy 2 bits, maximum singleton and proper-subset target mutual information 0 bits, and full-pattern target information 2 bits. Train and test surface identities are disjoint; target counts are exactly balanced; filler, position, nuisance, and competing-count checks pass. The confirmatory matrix has 11,696 examples per split, nuisance levels N0/N2/N3/N4, counts 0/2/4, aligned and randomized positions, and three independently initialized four-layer models. Every boundary records correctness, entropy, JS, representation covariance/effective rank, margin SNR, and decision/override timing.

\part{I. Layer composition and response stability}
\section{No-shortcut Dataset V2 and nested override}
All six balanced training families learn well above four-way chance over the complete held-out nuisance matrix. Mean accuracies are 0.913 for pairs, 0.885 for three-token sums, 0.908 for sparse pairs, 0.913 for functors, 0.890 for short nested rules, and 0.887 for long nested rules. The worst generator--seed--nuisance cell is 0.646. This minimum and the seed means (0.718, 0.993, and 0.994) reveal material optimization variability; we retain it rather than presenting only a pooled success.

Stable top-1 behavior occurs for 89.5\% of held-out identities. Among nested long-rule cases, 70.3\% eventually prefer the correct long target over the competing short-rule target and retain that preference. Thus the network often performs contextual override rather than merely repeating a locally valid suffix rule. The result is measurable but not universal.

\section{Nested-pattern bridge: causal reuse under a competence gate}
To separate behavioral override from internal reuse, we cross irrelevant, supportive, refining, override, and multi-level extensions; base lengths 2/3/4; extension lengths $+1/+2/+4$; aligned/randomized positions; and three seeds. Every modular core has zero singleton and proper-subset target MI and 2 bits of full-pattern information. Each long example is matched with its embedded short pattern, an unrelated same-target pattern, and a nonequivalent control. We record residual, block/SA/FF update, head-output, causal head-utility, and update-replacement relations at all six layers, with an untrained control.

Competence divides the result. Supportive extensions reach 0.999 final accuracy and irrelevant extensions 0.721, with aligned-minus-randomized gaps only 0.002 and 0.007. Refining, override, and multi-level variants reach 0.309, 0.293, and 0.350, respectively, near four-way chance. Their divergence, target crossing, head recruitment, and hierarchy geometry are therefore retained as failure diagnostics only. This negative gate does not contradict the narrower Dataset V2 nested result above: the earlier fixed short/long rule is competent, whereas the new simultaneous length/extension hierarchy is not.

Absolute residual cosine is not sufficient evidence: it averages 0.999 at random initialization because the query and absolute-position state are shared. The matched update contrast is more diagnostic. Training increases mean nested-minus-nonequivalent block-update similarity from 0.002 to 0.122. Within competent same-target strata, block-update cosine is 0.628; FF updates are more similar than SA updates (0.701 versus 0.504). The result supports partial reuse of a learned computational increment, not identity of the raw state.

Causal replacement gives the strongest bridge evidence. In competent same-target extensions, inserting the embedded-short donor into the long computation changes the intact distribution by 0.063 JS bits and flips final top-1 in 13.1\% of interventions. Unrelated same-target donors cost 0.079 bits and flip 15.0\%; nonequivalent donors cost 0.161 bits and flip 23.6\%. Causal head reuse is moderate rather than one-to-one: mean utility Spearman correlation is 0.264 and top-2 overlap 0.612. We therefore carry forward a narrow claim: a competent Transformer can reuse a causally substitutable short computation under target-preserving lexical extension. This is a controlled precursor to, not evidence of, semantic hierarchy.

\section{Length, predictive order, and the depth gate}
The next experiment separates three quantities that are easily conflated.  Raw pattern length $n$ counts visible pattern tokens; predictive order $p^*$ is the smallest subset size that determines the target; and architectural depth $D$ is the number of Transformer blocks.  For a depth boundary $l$, let $L_{\rm first}$ be the first correct top-1 boundary and $L_{\rm stable}$ the first boundary after which correctness persists.  The proposed theory asks whether $L_{\rm stable}$ is better described by $f(p^*)$ than by $f(n)$, but this comparison is meaningful only when the model has learned every relevant rule.

We construct lengths $n\in\{2,3,4,6,8\}$ in three regimes.  Redundant extensions keep a two-token modular core ($p^*=2$); supportive extensions add explicit target-compatible tokens ($p^*=1$ for $n>2$); necessary extensions use an $n$-way modular secret-sharing rule ($p^*=n$).  The corrected information audit is applied to the actual long pattern: redundant long patterns have a sufficient proper subset, supportive long patterns have 2-bit singleton cues, and necessary patterns have zero singleton and proper-subset MI but 2 bits jointly.  Contiguous/sparse layouts, aligned/randomized positions, and 0/2/4/8 nuisance tokens control span and distraction.

The preregistered eight-block smoke gate fails. Redundant accuracy remains 0.961--1.000 and supportive accuracy 0.994--1.000. Necessary accuracy is 0.996, 0.852, 0.500, 0.322, and 0.240 for $n=2,3,4,6,8$. Therefore necessary $n\geq4$ is excluded, and the $D\in\{2,4,6,8,12\}$ architecture sweep is not run. A deeper model recovering these cells could establish an architectural requirement; training deeper models only after the fixed smoke failure would instead confound capacity with competence.

\caption{Left: the cellwise competence phase diagram; the dashed line is the 0.70 gate. Right: stable decision boundary for qualified cells only. Failed necessary-pattern cells are never converted into depth evidence.}\end{figure}

Within the qualified target-preserving regimes, increasing $n$ does not produce a consistent delay: redundant mean stable boundaries range from 4.67 to 5.58, while supportive means range from 4.15 to 5.07. The descriptive raw-length fit explains only $R^2=0.016$. A $p^*$ fit reaches $R^2=0.701$, but this apparent advantage is not confirmatory: it uses one seed, one architecture, and only the competent $p^*\in\{1,2,3\}$ range. In particular, the high-$p^*$ cells needed to test the theory are precisely those that fail behaviorally. The defensible result is narrower: added context length need not demand later stabilization when sufficient predictive order stays fixed.

Update and head measurements refine that boundary rather than overturning it. Across lengths 3--8, mean block-update cosine with the length-2 computation is 0.32--0.37 for redundant and 0.42--0.49 for supportive extensions; FF similarity exceeds SA similarity. Mean top-2 head overlap remains roughly 0.51--0.58, implying distributed partial reuse with continuing recruitment, not a fixed head set. These are competent target-preserving comparisons. They do not show that necessary higher-order integration recruits later layers or additional heads.

\section{Pilot reproduction and generalization}
The prior fixed-depth result reproduces exactly: under pattern evidence and four distractors, target-probability variance is $0.0347\to0.0106\to0.0109$, JS dispersion is $0.0650\to0.0307\to0.0106$ bits, and within-example entropy is $1.326\to2.094\to2.277$ bits. In the new matrix, all three generators achieve 100\% final accuracy with eight nuisances. \section{Correct signal grows faster than margin fluctuation}
At eight nuisances, the aggregate mean correct-target margin changes from $-32.02$ logits initially to $+10.51$ finally, mean target probability from $1.4\times10^{-10}$ to 0.9998, and top-1 fraction from 0 to 1.  Each generator independently changes from negative to positive margin. This rules out the ``stable but uniformly wrong'' failure mode.

The stronger absolute-contraction hypothesis is nevertheless false here. Margin variance starts at zero because the aligned prediction-position state is initially identical across nuisance realizations; it finishes at 1.89. Final median margin SNR is $+10.24$. Thus reliability comes from roughly 42.5 logits of signal growth outpacing fluctuation growth, not from an SNR denominator collapse. Prediction-space JS can contract even while logit-margin variance expands because the softmax geometry and the mean decision direction both change.

\section{Decision formation, update covariance, and scaling non-results}
Mean first/stable top-1 depths are 3.00/3.40 boundaries at nuisance 0, 3.08/3.48 at nuisance 4, and 3.41/3.88 at nuisance 8. Mean settling delays are 0.48, 0.40, and 0.47; mean correctness reversals remain 1.37--1.52. Nuisance modestly delays decision formation, but a five-form comparison finds no scaling law: the best stable-depth fit has $R^2=0.013$ and held-out error 1.65 boundaries. Positive-margin SNR fits explain at most 1.0\% of variance; logarithmic and linear BIC differ by less than one. A power law is unsupported.

Mean boundary update is $+5.12$ logits and 73.7\% of family/seed/noise transition cells have positive mean progress. Median cumulative signal is $+40.39$ logits. Median summed marginal update variance is 3.55, but median summed off-diagonal covariance is $-2.12$, leaving actual cumulative fluctuation variance 0.51. Later updates therefore cancel a substantial part of earlier realization-specific fluctuation.

Only 7/27 family--seed trajectories contract monotonically; 11 expand then contract, five oscillate, three contract late, and one does not contract.  This favors cumulative nonlinear composition over a layerwise denoising story.

\part{II. Self-attention window and contextual transport}
\section{Reachability is necessary, not sufficient}
The mask tests verify exact graph reachability.  Unreachable long-range cells remain near four-way chance: accuracies are 0.188 for $(s,w,L)=(2,1,1)$, 0.234 for $(4,1,2)$, and 0.234 for $(8,2,2)$. Reachable $(2,1,2)$ reaches 0.797; $(4,1,4)$, $(8,4,2)$, and $(8,\mathrm{full},2)$ reach 1.0.  Yet reachable $(8,2,4)$ reaches only 0.078.  The causal cone is a necessary structural bound, not a sufficient learning law.

\section{Experiment 2.5: information exposure and variance attribution}
We next cross predictive span $s_G\in\{2,8,16\}$, nuisance distance $s_N\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$, effective window $w\in\{1,2,4,8,16,\mathrm{full}\}$, depths 2/4/6, two seeds, and three nuisance types. The target is the balanced rule $Y=(A+B)\bmod4$, for which neither token is predictive alone. N1 uses same-vocabulary random tokens, N2 partial predictive fragments, and N3 complete competing patterns. Full-attention models are evaluated under all truncated windows without retraining; three locally trained window controls distinguish immediate exposure from adaptation.

The first result falsifies a generic ``less is more'' account. Full-trained/full-evaluated models achieve 0.9995 accuracy with final margin variance 1.890. Merely truncating these models often destroys the learned routing: the graphically signal-reachable/nuisance-unreachable regime reaches only 0.411 accuracy. In contrast, locally trained restricted models reach 1.000 accuracy with mean variance 1.110. Thus $Lw\ge s_G$ states possibility, not learned functional transport, and training regime cannot be ignored.

\includegraphics[width=.48\linewidth]{docs/papers/paper0_5/figures/group25/g25_accuracy_surface.png}\hfill
\includegraphics[width=.48\linewidth]{docs/papers/paper0_5/figures/group25/g25_margin_variance_surface.png}
\caption{Full-trained models under inference-time window restriction. Accuracy and margin variance improve toward full exposure; reachability alone does not preserve the learned computation.}\end{figure}

Selective attention masking supplies the cleaner causal test. Blocking only nuisance key positions while retaining their embeddings preserves 1.000 accuracy, increases mean target margin by 0.229 logits, and reduces nuisance-conditioned margin variance from 1.864 to 1.109 (40.5\%). Blocking two matched random positions changes margin by only $-0.034$ logits, whereas blocking the two predictive positions reduces accuracy to 0.249 and raises variance to 67.40. SA therefore transports nuisance values that measurably perturb the decision variable, but excluding all distant context is not equivalent to excluding the causally irrelevant positions.

Variance attribution also rejects the claim that SA is the sole injector. Across the full intervention grid, mean sublayer changes are $+4.81$ for SA and $+4.49$ for FF, while normalization is associated with $-0.65$; these averages are dominated by failing truncated evaluations and must not be read as pure nuisance import. In the nonlinear descriptive model (architecture-cell holdout $R^2=0.959$), window, signal magnitude, layer, normalization change, and signal attention mass rank above nuisance attention mass and distance. Mean off-diagonal update covariance is negative; 86.8\% of valid cells have positive repair and the median repair fraction is 0.812. The supported mechanism is joint: SA expands accessible information, nonlinear SA/FF transformations amplify or rotate it, candidate competition changes the boundary, and later layers cancel much of the realization-specific deviation.

The bridge to bounded materialization is correspondingly narrow. Selective exclusion of known irrelevant context can improve SNR, but indiscriminate restriction can erase the learned signal path. ``Less'' helps only when the budget removes nuisance while respecting the model's trained routing.

\part{III. Heads and the SA contribution}
\section{Replacement, specialization, and interaction}
Across 16 layer/head cells, mean target-log-probability drops are 0.0316 nats for zero ablation, 0.0108 for mean replacement, 0.0006 for equivalent-family replacement, and 0.0194 for mismatched replacement.  Zero ablation increases within-family JS by $4.0\times10^{-5}$ bits and reduces between-family JS by $3.93\times10^{-4}$ on average. Mismatch produces the largest discrimination damage ($1.44\times10^{-3}$ bits).  Stability and discrimination are therefore related but non-identical head roles.

\section{Integrated interpretation}
The statistical view is supported in relative form: predictive-family JS contracts, correct margin becomes strongly positive, and signal grows faster than absolute margin fluctuation. The dynamical view is supported descriptively: decisions reverse, trajectories are usually non-monotone, cross-layer fluctuations anticorrelate, and cumulative JVP fidelity decreases with horizon. The information-theoretic premise is checked directly for no-shortcut and necessary constructions: singleton and proper-subset target MI vanish while the full pattern carries 2 bits. Redundant and supportive length controls deliberately violate the proper-subset condition in distinct, audited ways. Representation-space mutual information is still not estimated. The statistical-mechanical view is partial: correctness signal, fluctuation, $R$, $\chi$, and covariance are measured, but neither a universal scaling law nor a finite-size phase transition is supported.

The most economical account is \emph{state-dependent anisotropic refinement}. Layers transport, rotate, expand, and contract different perturbations; SA reachability constrains which distinctions can enter the prediction position, while training determines whether reachable paths are useful. Selective masking shows that nuisance access contributes causally to variance, but the window surface and attribution model show comparable FF changes, normalization effects, competition, and substantial later repair. Heads supply parallel, interacting vector-field components. No single layer or head need be a complete estimator, and final stabilization need not be monotone.

\section{Limitations and open tests}
Dataset V2 uses three seeds but only one architecture scale and four blocks; it cannot identify universal exponents. One seed is materially weaker, and nuisance count is not orthogonal to every surface statistic. The nested bridge passes competence only for target-preserving extensions; its refining/override/hierarchy measurements cannot support mechanism or Paper~0.6 hierarchy claims. The length/depth extension likewise uses one smoke seed and one eight-block architecture; necessary $n\geq4$ fails, so architectural minimum depth and high-order scaling remain unidentified. Head-role categories are causal summaries of this task, not stable semantics. Experiment~2.5 uses two seeds, three depths, and a sparse rather than full Cartesian grid. Its nuisance-key intervention is stronger than its descriptive attribution model, but the synthetic positions are known exactly and do not address how nuisance should be identified in natural text. Group~2 contains a reachable optimization failure. Group~3 has one seed/model and only one head-count point. Huge $R$ values from near-zero denominators are not treated as precise effect sizes.

\section{Override, training, and variance controls}
Answer-changing contexts are never labelled nuisance. In the legacy grid their JS contraction is $-0.0328$ bits (CI $[-0.0543,-0.0141]$), weaker than irrelevant-nuisance contraction $-0.0474$ ($[-0.0778,-0.0217]$). Random checkpoints have low dispersion only because target signal is absent. Training-time SA/FF trajectories are non-monotone, and stable attention motifs do not predict causal SA utility. These negative controls constrain the theory rather than being discarded.

\section{Relationship to later continual-learning work}
Only here, after the mechanistic results, do we state the downstream implication. Stable predictive equivalence could become a diagnostic precursor for future adaptation, but Paper~0.5 neither implements nor validates continual learning. Common-direction addition is not beneficial, on-manifold controls weaken label-specific transfer, and the separate natural held-out utility gate remains negative. Persistent prototypes or adapters therefore remain blocked pending matched-budget future utility, override preservation, contamination controls, and rollback.

\section{Conclusion}
Depth transforms no-shortcut predictive structures into stable correct responses across six controlled formats, often through non-monotone composition and measurable contextual override. In competent target-preserving nested extensions, matched update similarity and causal replacement show partial reuse of the embedded short computation; broader refinement and hierarchy remain competence-blocked. Correct signal grows faster than nuisance-conditioned fluctuation; absolute margin variance need not contract. SA reachability does not guarantee learned transport. Selective nuisance removal reduces variance without damaging signal, yet indiscriminate truncation harms full-trained models; SA and FF both alter variance and later layers repair much of it. Cumulative JVPs retain local directional information while norm accuracy deteriorates. Heads contribute in distributed, non-additive ways. Together these findings turn ``layer refinement'' into a competence-gated theory of predictive ensembles, nested reuse, causal cones, information exposure, and interacting residual updates while preserving its failures and boundary conditions.
